# Independent scientific review of the locked milling-selection replay

Review date: 20 November 2026. Review scope: `outputs/run_a`, the frozen protocol, event-based verification, six core aggregate tables, and additional direct calculations from saved per-pool results and event logs. No frozen scientific source, policy, protocol, or cohort was changed. Outcome separation was released only after the run freeze was created.

## Supported conclusion

The primary two-batch procedure added no measured value over its matched no-update procedure or the locked mixed-model static benchmark at the 25% entry budget. This is an exact observed equality in every one of 188 Uruguay temporal pools and 62 primary Arkansas pools, not merely a nonsignificant mean difference. The mixed-model static benchmark also selected the same ranking as paddy yield alone. The defensible result is a strong simple rule within the Uruguay observed-answer cohort, weaker recovery at the same budget in Arkansas, and no demonstrated value from the specified feedback update at the primary budget. It is not a claim that adaptive phenotyping in general is ineffective, nor that every secondary-budget effect was null.

## Feedback, secondary selection, and budgets

At the primary budget, the primary update changed neither untested ranks, tested membership, nor shortlisted membership relative to no-update in either primary cohort. Its static equivalence has a structural explanation: the final mixed model uses only relative yield and yield percentile, with positive coefficients for both, so its expected-output ranking is the yield ranking. It does not provide an additional learned ranking advantage over yield alone. The uncertainty score includes shared pool variability and is a historical variability heuristic, not a calibrated joint uncertainty distribution over ranks.

The pre-evaluation secondary trees configuration must remain separate. In Uruguay it had 0.230037% mean regret versus 0.222186% for its own static/no-update ranking; the feedback-minus-no-update contrast was +0.007850 percentage points (descriptive interval 0 to +0.022941). Feedback worsened one pool (`UY__2017__UEPL__E5-1`) by 1.475843 percentage points and tied the other 187. Its mean difference from the locked mixed benchmark was +0.003950 points (−0.003790 to +0.013140), offering no adaptive improvement.

In Arkansas, the secondary trees procedure had 2.519653% regret versus 2.597324% for mixed: −0.077672 points (−0.153057 to 0), arising from two improved pools and 60 ties. Exactly the same improvement was obtained by its static/no-update ordering. Its feedback contrast was zero in every external pool. This is a small descriptive ranking difference, not evidence of a benefit from paid feedback, and it must not be promoted to the primary result after evaluation. The secondary update changed untested ranks in 140/188 Uruguay and 26/62 Arkansas pools, changed tested membership in seven and one respectively, and changed the final shortlist only in the one adversely affected Uruguay pool. Rank movement alone therefore does not establish decision value.

The locked primary matched no-update at every budget. At the 10% design budget, its first-batch allocation differed from static mixed and increased mean regret by 0.009326 percentage points in Uruguay and 0.129535 points in Arkansas; differences from static at the other tested budgets were zero. Thus, a blanket claim that every two-batch result equals static at every budget would be incorrect.

## Required limitations and reporting safeguards

1. The oracle is conditional on available measured answers. Historical testing and later-report recoverability can select candidates. Coverage diagnostics do not identify outcomes for omitted entries. The 2017–2020 Arkansas historical-cell subset must stay distinguished from the 2021–2025 contemporary-table subset.
2. Uruguay's rough-rice denominator has same-program methods support, not archive-wide certification, and its moisture convention is unresolved. Use a source-basis output index and within-pool relative regret. Arkansas's 45-lb bushel and 12% moisture conversion must not be replaced by a generic 13% commercial convention. Relative regret cancels a common pool-wide conversion, not candidate-specific measurement differences.
3. The product of separately averaged yield and recovery is not necessarily the mean of matched-plot products. The fixed-query sensitivity assesses endpoint sensitivity; it does not rerun adaptive decisions under an alternate feedback measurement definition. Shared physical assays and variance of candidate means remain unidentified.
4. Four Uruguay harvest years provide little temporal independence. Year resampling does not remove repeated sites, lines, correlated trial stages, changing pool composition, or program-level differences. The zero-to-zero bootstrap interval for an identical policy contrast is a consequence of identical observed decisions, not a prospective equivalence guarantee or evidence of unlimited precision.
5. The common input set intentionally contains only two yield-derived variables. It excludes potentially useful grain classes, genotype histories, markers, quality measurements, and richer field covariates. Failure of the frozen update cannot establish that those unavailable or unharmonized inputs would have no value. Training uses candidate observations, whereas evaluation weights pools equally; the random intercept does not make all fitting observations independent.
6. Recovery forecasts are diagnostically imperfect despite useful rankings. The mixed model's candidate-weighted recovery bias was −4.6521 percentage points in Uruguay and +3.2275 in Arkansas; RMSE was 7.0231 and 10.4461 points. This reinforces the distinction between ranking utility, forecast calibration, and transferable recovery prediction. The yield-only artificial prediction of 100 is only a ranking surrogate.
7. Source-rounded ties can alter measured oracle membership near k. Arkansas half-unit envelopes describe deterministic rounding ambiguity, not sampling confidence. A small secondary static improvement involving two pools warrants particular caution. Previously exposed and source-compromised pools remain secondary, with no opportunity to repair the primary conclusion by pooling them in.
8. Sequential phenotyping, uncertainty-based acquisition, and ranking-based genotype selection already have close prior art. The publishable contribution, if pursued, is the documented postharvest decision, source-linked replay, exact feedback ablation, and evidence that source-program testing performance does not carry over at the same budget. Do not claim a new general acquisition method or first sequential selection study.

## Review judgment

The results support a coherent, bounded negative-result paper about the limited value of this feedback update at the primary budget and the domain dependence of a simple static rule. They do not support an adaptive-superiority paper, a universal testing-budget recommendation, a prospective equivalence claim, or operational cost savings. No scientific implementation defect was found in the completed core verifications. At the initial review, submission readiness still depended on the separate clean repeat, completed sensitivity assessment, source-linked final manuscript, and rendered artifact review; these are completion checks rather than reasons to retune the experiment. The final scientific review below supersedes those initial pending statements within its stated scope.
